Week 2 · Lesson 01 · AI for Lawyers

The AI-augmented lawyer.

AI will not replace lawyers. But a lawyer who builds durable workflows around AI will out-research, out-draft and out-advise the one who does everything by hand. This lesson is not about a tool or a prompt. It is about a working relationship — where AI drafts at speed, and the lawyer verifies, and decides, and remains the one who is accountable to the client and the court.

01 / 04

AI drafts, the lawyer verifies and decides

Before any prompt, learn the one rule that keeps the rest of this week honest: AI is a fast, tireless drafting partner. It is not co-counsel, not an authority on the law, and never accountable. That last word carries the weight of the profession — accountability runs to the client, the court and the bar, and it cannot be delegated to a machine.

Think of AI the way a supervising partner thinks of a bright first-year associate who never sleeps and has never been sanctioned — because it has never been responsible for anything. Ask for a first draft of a demand letter and you get one in seconds. Ask for the elements of a claim, three arguments for a motion, or a plain-language summary of a forty-page agreement, and it produces all of them. This is genuinely useful: the blank page and the cold read are the most expensive minutes in legal work, and AI compresses both. But that associate has not read the actual file, cannot open the binding authority in your jurisdiction, will invent a case that sounds perfect and does not exist, and states a wrong proposition of law with the same fluent confidence it uses for a right one.

So the workflow is always two-handed. One hand generates — issues, drafts, arguments, first passes, summaries. The other hand verifies and decides — what the law actually says, what the file actually shows, what strategy serves this client, what is safe to put in front of a court. The generating hand is fast and cheap. The verifying-and-deciding hand is slow, expert, and the thing the client is actually paying for. Every stage of legal work runs on this split. Why the line never moves

Notice that across all five stages, the verifying-and-deciding hand keeps the same jobs: confirming the authority is real and still good law, judging what the file actually supports, owning the advice, and standing behind every word that reaches a client or a court. AI's share grows as the tools improve — but that column of human judgment is the practice of law itself. A lawyer who guards it well can adopt any tool without fear; one who surrenders it has, in the eyes of the client and the bar, stopped practising.

02 / 04

A lawyer's day, reclaimed

The promise of AI in legal work is not "less lawyering." It is less of the low-value, often non-billable, judgment-free labour that surrounds lawyering — so that more of the day can go to the part clients actually value and pay for: analysis, strategy and counsel.

Most lawyers lose hours every day to work that is necessary but not the point: reformatting a document, summarising a long email chain, assembling a first-draft chronology, retyping boilerplate clauses, drafting the routine cover letter for the twentieth time. Much of it is hard to bill and none of it requires your training; all of it steals from the part that does. AI is at its best exactly here — on the high-volume, template-shaped, first-pass tasks that drain a day without moving a single matter forward on the merits.

Reclaimed, not erased

The honest version of this figure: AI does not hand a lawyer a shorter day for free. It gives a better-spent one — and it quietly reshapes what is billable. The hours it saves on formatting and boilerplate are hours you reinvest in high-value analysis and counsel, or in going home. A caution worth naming now: efficiency changes billing, and the profession is still working out how the value of AI-assisted work is fairly charged. Reclaimed time only helps the client if you are honest about how it is captured.

03 / 04

Should AI touch this task?

Not every task is a safe place for AI. Before you delegate anything, run it through the gate below — but note first that one dial is not a dial at all. Client confidentiality and privilege are a hard veto: if a task exposes protected information to a tool that is not approved and compliant, the answer is no, regardless of how low the stakes or how easy the check.

The confidentiality veto comes first, on purpose. Pasting privileged client material into a general, non-approved AI tool can waive privilege, breach your duty of confidentiality, and expose the data to training or disclosure. No efficiency gain survives that. Only once the veto is clear do the other dials matter. Stakes: how much rides on getting this exactly right — a first-draft internal summary is low, a filing or a client opinion is high. Verifiability: how easily you can check the output yourself — you can confirm a clause against a template in minutes; you cannot casually verify a citation without pulling the actual authority.

Set the veto and move the two dials to describe a real task, then read the verdict. The pattern to internalise: AI is safest on non-confidential, low-stakes, high-verifiability work — and categorically off-limits the moment protected client information would touch a non-compliant tool. When in doubt, the machine drafts on de-identified or public material and you verify; it never decides, and it never sees data it shouldn't.

The rule under the dials

High stakes plus low verifiability is the danger zone for hallucinated authorities and wrong law — and confidentiality is the trapdoor beneath the whole floor. The safe move is never a flat "don't use AI." It is: clear the confidentiality veto first, then let AI draft the structure on de-identified or public material, and put your own verified judgment — real, good-law authority; a file you have actually read — on top before anything reaches a client, an opponent, or a court.

04 / 04

Four myths, flipped

Every conversation about AI in the profession carries some version of these four beliefs — two from the boosters, two from the sceptics. Flip each card to see the more honest version. Neither breathless hype nor flat refusal survives contact with a real matter, a real client, or a real judge.

The goal of this lesson is not to make you enthusiastic or fearful, but accurate. AI in legal work is a powerful drafting tool with real, named limitations — confidentiality and privilege risk, hallucinated authorities, and embedded bias — that you manage the way you manage any professional risk: by knowing exactly where it lives and refusing to look away from it.

More honestly

AI has no duty to a client, no standing before a court, and no accountability to the bar. It replaces tasks, not lawyers. The lawyer who uses it well spends less of the day on drudgery and more of it on judgment, strategy and counsel — the work clients actually pay for.

"If it cites a case, it's real"

More honestly

AI hallucinates — it invents case names, citations, quotations and holdings in the same confident voice it uses for real ones. Fluency is not authority. Every citation must be pulled, read, and confirmed as real and still good law before it enters a filing. Courts have sanctioned lawyers for this.

"It's neutral and objective"
A machine has no bias and applies the law evenly.
tap to flip
More honestly

AI learned from human text and carries its biases — in the outcomes it predicts, the arguments it favours, the parties it frames sympathetically. Used unchecked in areas like risk-scoring or drafting, it can quietly encode unfairness. You supply the judgment and the equity; it does not.

"Serious lawyers refuse it"
Using AI is a shortcut — sloppy, even unethical.
tap to flip
More honestly

Refusing a drafting tool to spend billable evenings retyping boilerplate is not diligence — it is lost time the client may be paying for. Diligence is verifying every authority and owning every decision. Let AI draft the structure on safe material; keep every judgment that matters firmly in human hands.

The one-sentence version

The AI-augmented lawyer lets the machine draft at speed on non-confidential, checkable, lower-stakes work — and keeps every decision, every verified authority, and every duty to the client and the court firmly in human hands.

What to carry forward

You now have the working relationship at the heart of this week. AI is a fast drafting partner with real limits; you are the accountable professional. Clear the confidentiality veto first, run every task through the delegation gate, verify every authority before it reaches a court, and never let a confident sentence stand in for a checked one.
