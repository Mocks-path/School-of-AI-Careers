Week 1 · Lesson 01 · AI for Teachers

The AI-augmented teacher.

AI will not replace teachers. But a teacher who builds durable workflows around AI will out-plan, out-differentiate and out-recover the one who does everything by hand. This lesson is not about a tool. It is about a working relationship — where AI drafts at speed, and the teacher decides with judgment.

01 / 04

AI drafts, the teacher decides

Before any prompt, learn the one rule that keeps the rest of this course honest: AI is a fast, tireless drafting partner. It is not a colleague, not an authority, and never accountable. That last word matters — accountability is yours, and it cannot be delegated to a machine.

Think of AI the way a busy editor thinks of a junior writer who never sleeps. Ask for ten warm-up questions and you get ten in seconds. Ask for a parent email in three tones and it produces all three. This is genuinely useful: the blank page is the most expensive minute in teaching, and AI abolishes it. But the junior writer has never met your students, does not know which child had a hard week, cannot feel when an example will land or wound, and will state a wrong fact with the same confidence as a right one.

So the workflow is always two-handed. One hand generates — options, drafts, variations, first passes. The other hand decides — what fits this class, this child, this standard, this moment. The generating hand is fast and cheap. The deciding hand is slow, expert and irreplaceable. Every stage of a teacher's week runs on this split. Why the line never moves

Notice that across all five stages, the deciding hand keeps the same jobs: judging fit, protecting a child's dignity, verifying facts, owning the grade and the message. AI's share grows as the tools improve — but that column of human decisions is the profession itself. A teacher who guards it well can adopt any tool without fear; one who surrenders it has already stopped teaching.

02 / 04

A teacher's week, reclaimed

The promise of AI in teaching is not "less teaching." It is less of the invisible, unpaid, joyless labour that surrounds teaching — so that more of the week can go to the part only a human can do: being present with students.

Most teachers lose hours every week to work that is necessary but not the point: reformatting a worksheet, writing the fifteenth "please see me" email, drafting a rubric from scratch, retyping notes into a newsletter. None of it requires your training; all of it steals from the part that does. AI is at its best exactly here — on the high-volume, low-stakes, template-shaped tasks that drain a week without improving a single lesson.

Reclaimed, not erased

The honest version of this figure: AI does not give a teacher a shorter week. It gives a better-spent one. The two or three hours it saves on formatting and boilerplate are hours you choose to reinvest — in a struggling reader, a richer discussion, or simply in going home on time. The machine handles the tasks that never deserved your degree, so your degree can go where it belongs.

03 / 04

Should AI touch this task?

Not every task is a safe place for AI. Before you delegate anything, run it through three questions — the stakes, the privacy, and the verifiability. This one habit prevents nearly every serious mistake teachers make with these tools.

The three dials work together. Stakes: how much rides on getting this exactly right — a warm-up question is low, a report-card grade or an IEP note is high. Privacy: how much identifiable student information the task requires — a generic quiz is safe, anything naming a real child and their struggles is not, unless your school has an approved, compliant tool. Verifiability: how easily you can check the output yourself — you can eyeball a vocabulary list in seconds; you cannot easily verify a "fact" about a historical event you half-remember.

Move the three dials to describe a real task and read the verdict. The pattern to internalise: AI is safest on low-stakes, low-privacy, high-verifiability work — and most dangerous when all three point the other way. When in doubt, the machine drafts and you verify; it never decides and it never sees data it shouldn't.

The rule under the dials

High stakes plus low verifiability plus real student data is the danger zone — and it is exactly where the confident-sounding wrong answer does the most harm. The safe move is never "don't use AI." It is: strip the private details, let AI draft the structure, and put your own verified judgment on top before anything reaches a student, a parent, or a permanent record.

04 / 04

Four myths, flipped

Every staffroom conversation about AI carries some version of these four beliefs — two from the boosters, two from the sceptics. Flip each card to see the more honest version. Neither breathless hype nor flat refusal survives contact with a real classroom.

The goal of this lesson is not to make you enthusiastic or fearful, but accurate. AI in teaching is a powerful drafting tool with real, named limitations — hallucination, bias, and privacy risk — that you manage the same way you manage any other classroom risk: by knowing where it lives and refusing to look away from it.

More honestly

AI has no relationship with a child, no accountability, and no read of the room. It replaces tasks, not teachers. The teacher who uses it well simply spends less of the week on drudgery and more of it teaching.

More honestly

AI hallucinates — it invents facts, citations and dates in the same confident voice it uses for correct ones. Fluency is not accuracy. Anything factual that reaches a student must be verified by you first.

"It's neutral and objective"

More honestly

AI learned from human text and carries its biases — in the names it favours, the examples it reaches for, the voices it centres. Used unchecked, it can quietly narrow whose stories your class sees. You curate; it does not.

More honestly

Refusing a drafting tool to spend your evenings on formatting is not integrity — it is lost time. Integrity is deciding what reaches your students. Let AI draft the boilerplate; keep every judgment that matters.

The one-sentence version

The AI-augmented teacher lets the machine draft at speed on low-stakes, non-private, checkable work — and keeps every decision that touches a child's learning, dignity or record firmly in human hands.

What to carry forward

You now have the working relationship at the heart of this course. AI is a fast drafting partner with real limits; you are the accountable professional. Run every task through the delegation gate, reinvest the reclaimed hours in your students, and never let a confident sentence stand in for a verified one.
