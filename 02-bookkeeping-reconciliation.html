Week 5 · Lesson 02 · AI for Accountants

Bookkeeping, reconciliation & data, verified.

This is where AI earns its keep — and where it is most dangerous. Capture, extraction and categorisation are the grind of every close, and AI can compress them dramatically. But AI misreads amounts, invents totals, and applies the wrong code in the same confident voice it uses when it is right. So the whole workflow hinges on one gate that never moves: reconcile to source. Nothing AI produces is real until a human has traced it back to the document it came from and recomputed what matters. AI extracts and proposes; the accountant reconciles, verifies and signs.

A note before we begin. This is professional education about bookkeeping and reconciliation workflows — it is jurisdiction-neutral and is not accounting, tax or audit advice, and it does not tell you how to treat any transaction. Nothing here recommends a specific tool. Never place identifiable client financial data into a consumer AI tool, and never let an AI-extracted figure enter the ledger, a return or a report without reconciling it to its source document.
01 / 03

The workflow, and the gate that never moves

Bookkeeping with AI is a short pipeline: capture the document, extract the fields, categorise the transaction, reconcile to source, then verify or flag. Four of those steps are places AI can genuinely help. One of them — reconcile to source — is a hard human gate that every figure must pass through before it counts. Step through the flow below and watch that gate light up.

It is tempting to treat AI extraction as "done" the moment the fields appear on screen. They look complete, they look precise, and they arrive in seconds. But an AI reading of an invoice is a proposal, not a fact — a fast, plausible first draft that may contain a misread amount, a wrong tax code, or a total it computed unreliably. The reconcile-to-source gate is what turns a proposal into a booked entry. It is the single most important habit in this lesson: before any AI-extracted figure enters the ledger, it is traced back to the source document, and any figure the arithmetic depends on is recomputed — never trusted from the model. Why the gate is non-negotiable

Capture, Extract and Categorize are speed steps — AI is good at them, and getting better. Verify/flag is where you record the outcome and raise anything unresolved with the client. But Reconcile to source is the load-bearing wall of the whole workflow, and it is human by definition. It is the moment the profession's oldest discipline — tie every entry back to evidence — meets the AI era's newest risk: fluent, confident, wrong numbers. Skip the gate and the errors in the next figure flow straight into the books. Guard it, and AI becomes a genuine accelerator you can trust.

Notice how the errors hide. The mis-read amount is off by a digit that is easy to skim past. The tax code is plausible but wrong for this line. And the total is simply invented — it does not equal the sum of the lines, because the model predicted a likely-looking number rather than actually adding. None of these announce themselves; all three read as confidently as the correct fields. That is the point: AI extraction is a starting draft to be reviewed against the source, never a finished record. Catch all three, then read the verdict.

What the review taught

Three errors, three failure modes. The mis-read amount is an OCR-style slip — a 4 seen as a 9 — the kind that survives a casual glance. The wrong tax code is a categorisation error with real consequences for a return. And the hallucinated total is the signature AI failure: a fluent number that was never actually computed. The discipline that catches all three is the same one: compare every AI figure against the source, and recompute anything the total depends on. AI extraction saves you the typing; it never saves you the checking.

Reconcile to source — never trust AI arithmetic

The ledger and the bank statement should agree. AI has offered a tidy reconciliation and even a helpful "balanced ✓" — but there is a discrepancy hiding in the figures, and the AI's own running total is wrong because it was predicted, not added. Click the ledger line that does not tie back to the bank, then recompute the true difference yourself.

This is the everyday heart of bookkeeping: two sources that must reconcile, and a difference you must find and explain. AI can lay the two sides side by side and flag obvious mismatches — genuinely useful. But it will also confidently assert a total that is wrong, and declare a set of numbers "balanced" when they are not, because it approximated the arithmetic instead of performing it. The rule is absolute: never trust AI arithmetic on a figure that matters. Recompute it yourself or in a checked tool, and reconcile every line to its source. The habit that never retires

The Supplies line was booked at 960.00 but the bank shows 690.00 — a 270.00 discrepancy that must be chased to its source and explained, not smoothed over. And the AI's "balanced ✓, total 3,180.00" was doubly false: the sides do not agree, and neither even sums to 3,180. This is exactly why AI is never the source of record. It is a fast pair of eyes for laying the two sides side by side — and a liability the moment you trust its arithmetic or its verdict. You reconcile to source, you recompute the numbers that matter, and you decide when the books are right.

What to carry forward

Bookkeeping and reconciliation are where AI saves the most time and hides the most risk. Let it capture, extract and categorise at speed — then send every figure through the gate that never moves: reconcile to source. Review each AI extraction against the document it came from, catch the misread amounts, wrong codes and hallucinated totals, and never trust AI arithmetic on a figure that matters — recompute it. Keep identifiable client financial data out of consumer tools. AI extracts and proposes; you reconcile, verify and sign.
