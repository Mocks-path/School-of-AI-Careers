Week 5 · AI for Accountants · Lesson 03

The model calculates. The accountant signs.

Analysis, reporting and advisory are where accounting stops being bookkeeping and starts being judgement — where numbers turn into a story a business acts on. AI can pull data faster, spot patterns, and draft commentary in seconds. But it also invents figures, gets arithmetic subtly wrong, and manufactures confident explanations for things that never happened. So the workflow that matters here is not "let AI write the report" — it is a disciplined loop where AI supports and calculates, and the accountant verifies every figure to primary source and signs. AI is decision-support. It is never the source of record.

01 / 04

The analysis-and-reporting loop

Good financial analysis is not a single act; it is a loop that starts with a sharp question and ends with advice a client can act on. AI has a real role at every stage — but a different, bounded one at each. The durable skill is knowing exactly where the model helps and where it must hand back to you. The rule that runs through all five stages: AI supports and calculates; the accountant verifies every figure against primary source and signs.

Click each stage in the loop to see AI's role beside the accountant's — and the verification step that never disappears. Notice that "verify every figure" is not one stage among five; it is present in all of them. A model can accelerate the loop, but it cannot close it. Only a named human, accountable for the numbers, can do that.

Figure 1 · The analysis & reporting loop — AI's role vs yours

The loop is deliberately circular. Advice that goes out becomes the next question that comes back — did the cost-cutting work, did the forecast hold, what changed. AI keeps this loop turning quickly, which is genuinely valuable when a client needs an answer this week rather than next month. But speed is only an asset if the figures underneath are true. A fast loop built on an unverified number just spreads a wrong conclusion faster. The accountant's signature is the promise that the loop was closed with checked figures, not merely with plausible ones.

02 / 04

Reviewing AI-drafted commentary

Variance commentary — explaining why the numbers moved — is exactly the kind of writing AI drafts well and dangerously. Well, because it turns a table into readable prose in seconds. Dangerously, because it will confidently state a growth rate it never computed, carry forward a subtotal that doesn't add up, and invent a cause-and-effect story with no support in the data. The draft reads beautifully. That is the trap. The accountant's job is to review it as a skeptic, catching what the model got wrong before it goes out under the firm's name.

Below is an AI-drafted variance commentary. Three things in it are wrong, highlighted in yellow. Work through the review, catching each one against the actual figures — a hallucinated growth percentage, a subtotal that doesn't reconcile, and a causal claim the data never supports. This is professional skepticism made concrete: every figure verified to primary source, every claim tested, before a single word is sent.

Notice that none of the three errors looked wrong. A 42% figure reads as precise and authoritative; a subtotal in clean round thousands looks reconciled; a causal sentence about pricing power sounds like exactly the insight a client pays for. That is why the review cannot be a skim. AI does arithmetic unreliably and manufactures causation from correlation, so every number is re-derived to source and every "because" is challenged. The draft saved you the blank page. It did not save you the responsibility for what the page says.

03 / 04

False precision and spurious causation

Two failure modes show up again and again in AI-drafted analysis, and both are seductive because they sound authoritative. False precision is a number stated to more decimal places than the data can support — certainty manufactured out of noise. Spurious causation is a confident "X caused Y" drawn from two lines that merely moved together. A good analyst caveats; a language model, left unchecked, asserts. The fix is not to distrust every output — it is to convert overconfident claims into verified, properly hedged ones.

Toggle between the two versions of the same finding below. The first is how an unchecked model tends to phrase it: precise, causal, certain. The second is how a professional writes it after verifying the figures and thinking about what the data can and cannot prove. Same underlying numbers, radically different reliability — because one is a guess dressed as a fact, and the other is a checked statement that owns its uncertainty.

Figure 3 · Overconfident AI claim vs verified, caveated statement
◇ Overconfident — do not send

The verified version is less exciting to read, and that is precisely the point. Advisory work lives or dies on trust, and a client who catches you asserting a cause you can't defend — or a decimal you can't support — stops believing the rest of the report too. The properly caveated statement is not weaker; it is stronger, because every word in it will survive scrutiny. AI can help you draft either version. The difference between them is the verification and judgement you add — the part that is your name on the file, not the model's.

04 / 04

Analysis and advisory, kept honest

Analysis and reporting is where AI feels most magical — a table becomes a narrative in seconds — and therefore where the discipline must be strictest. The magic is in the drafting speed. The danger is that a fluent draft hides a false number, a broken subtotal, or an invented cause. The whole of advisory rests on figures that are true, so the whole of the workflow rests on verifying them.

The line is the one running through the entire week: AI supports and calculates, the accountant verifies and signs. Use it to pull and reshape data you then reconcile to source, to draft commentary you then review as a skeptic, to explore possible explanations you then test against the evidence. What it must never become is the source of record, the unquestioned author of a client's numbers, or a reason to send a report you haven't checked line by line. The four practices below keep AI in the support role where it belongs.

Question

Sharpen, then own it

Let AI help frame the analytical question and suggest angles. Then decide what the client actually needs to know. The question shapes everything downstream, so it stays a human judgement, not a model's default.

Verify

Every figure to source

AI pulls and calculates, but it hallucinates numbers and does arithmetic unreliably. Re-derive every figure against the primary record. A report is only as trustworthy as its least-checked number.

Review

Draft, then challenge

Have AI draft the commentary, then read it as a skeptic — hunting invented growth rates, broken subtotals, and unsupported causes. The blank page is the model's to fill; the meaning is yours to defend.

Advise

Caveat and sign

Turn overconfident, causal, over-precise claims into verified, hedged statements you can stand behind. Then sign. AI is decision-support; the advice and the accountability are the accountant's.

The honest limits, one more time

Everything in this lesson depends on the same guardrails. Hallucinated figures: AI will state a growth rate it never computed and a subtotal that doesn't add up, phrased with total confidence — verify every number to primary source, always. Unreliable arithmetic: language models predict text, they do not compute; a figure that looks calculated may simply be plausible, so re-derive it. Spurious causation: a model will assert a cause from a coincidence — challenge every "because" against the evidence before it becomes advice. Client confidentiality: a client's financial data is confidential; never paste identifiable figures into a consumer AI tool — keep the numbers in your sanctioned systems and work with de-identified or placeholder data. Source of record: AI is decision-support, never the record itself; the ledger, the working papers, and your signature are the record. AI supports and calculates. The accountant verifies every figure and signs. That is what turns a fast draft into a report a business can trust.

What to carry forward

Analysis, reporting and advisory is where AI gives accountants the most leverage — and where a fluent draft can hide a false number most easily. Let AI pull data, spot patterns, and draft commentary; then verify every figure to primary source, challenge every cause, caveat every claim, and sign only what you have checked. AI is decision-support and never the source of record.

  • Run the loop · question, pull & verify, analyse, draft, advise — AI supports each stage, you close it
  • Review as a skeptic · catch invented growth rates, broken subtotals, and unsupported causes before they send
  • Kill false precision · a decimal you can't support is a guess dressed as a fact
  • Challenge every "because" · correlation is not causation, and a model asserts what an analyst caveats
  • AI calculates, the accountant verifies and signs · the numbers, the advice, and the accountability are yours
