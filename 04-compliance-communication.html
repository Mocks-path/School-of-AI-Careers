Week 5 · AI for Accountants · Lesson 04

The tool drafts. The accountant verifies and signs.

Compliance, client communication and practice growth are where AI most obviously gives accountants their busy-season hours back — chasing documents, drafting client emails, summarising rules, clearing admin. But it is also where the two hardest failures live: a model that states a tax rule or rate with total confidence and gets it wrong, and a habit of pasting a client's confidential financial data into a tool that was never meant to hold it. The workflow that keeps a practice safe is simple to say and demanding to keep: every rule verified to a primary source, every client's data protected behind a firewall, and a human sign-off on everything. AI supports and calculates. The accountant verifies and signs.

01 / 04

Hours reclaimed across busy season

You can't reclaim time you haven't measured. Before reaching for AI, list the recurring tasks that quietly consume a busy-season week — then estimate, honestly, how much of each is first-draft, chasing, or organising work a model could accelerate under your review. Reclaimed time is only a win if it goes back into the judgement and client relationships that need you, not into rushing work out the door unchecked.

Two honest caveats. First, AI drafting and organising adds a verification step, so the "after" bars are never zero — a compliance return, a rule summary, or a client email sent unread goes out under your name and your licence, and a wrong one damages a client and your standing. If the hours you save get spent filing faster without checking, you've automated the wrong thing.

02 / 04

Verify the rule against a primary source

There is a now-famous failure in the legal world: professionals who cited AI-generated case law that did not exist, because the model produced confident, plausible, entirely fabricated citations. The same failure is waiting for accountants — but with tax rules, rates, thresholds, and filing deadlines. A model will state a provision, a percentage, or a due date with perfect fluency and get it wrong: an outdated rate, a threshold from the wrong year, or a relief that was never in the code at all. The only defence is the same one lawyers learned the hard way — verify every rule against the primary source before you rely on it.

Below, an AI assistant has stated a tax rule to support a client answer. It sounds authoritative and specific. Choose whether to take it on trust or verify it against the primary source. One path is how practices get into trouble; the other is the only defensible workflow. The figures and provision here are illustrative — the discipline is not.

Figure 2 · An AI-stated tax rule — trust it, or verify it?
AI assistant · answering a client eligibility question ◇ Unverified output
"Your client qualifies. Under the Small Enterprise Relief provision, businesses below the €95,000 turnover threshold may claim an accelerated 40% first-year allowance on qualifying equipment for the 2026 tax year. I'd recommend they proceed on that basis."

The details were plausible because the model is built to produce plausible text, not correct text — a specific threshold, a specific rate, a specific year all lend false authority. That is exactly why a confident answer is not evidence. Whether a provision exists, still applies this year, and applies to this client is a question only the primary source — the statute, the tax authority's current guidance, the official rate table — can answer. AI can point you toward what to check and draft the explanation once you've confirmed it. It cannot be the authority. Verify the rule, then advise. Never the other way around.

03 / 04

The confidentiality firewall and the sign-off gate

A client's financial data is confidential — a professional and often legal obligation, not a courtesy. Pasting identifiable client figures into a consumer AI tool can breach that confidentiality, your engagement terms, and data-protection law, because you do not control where that data goes or how it is retained. The durable habit is a firewall: identifiable client data stays inside your sanctioned systems, and only de-identified or placeholder information ever reaches an external tool. And beyond the firewall sits the gate that never moves — a human verifies and signs.

Even once data is safely de-identified, the model's answer is a draft. The accountant re-attaches it to the real client, verifies every figure and rule to source, and signs. AI supports and calculates; the accountant verifies and signs.

Two disciplines, working together. The firewall protects the client: identifiable financial data stays behind it, and de-identification is not optional politeness but a condition of using external tools at all. The sign-off gate protects the work: even a well-scoped, de-identified query produces a draft, not a decision, so a qualified human re-connects it to the real client, verifies every figure and rule, and signs. Neither substitutes for the other. Strip the identity going out, verify the substance coming back, and put your name on it only when both are true.

04 / 04

Compliance and communication, kept honest

Compliance and client communication is where AI gives back the most visible busy-season hours — and where two failures can quietly cost a practice its standing: an unverified rule and a leaked client figure. The temptation is to let convenience creep past verification and past confidentiality. The discipline is to keep AI on the drafting and chasing, keep every rule tied to a primary source, keep client data behind the firewall, and sign nothing you haven't checked.

The line is the same one running through the whole week: AI supports and calculates, the accountant verifies and signs. Use it to draft the client email you'll personalise, to chase the missing document, to summarise a rule you'll then confirm to source, to clear the admin that never needed judgement. What it must never become is the authority on a tax rule, a place you paste confidential client data, or a reason to file something under your name unread. The four practices below keep AI in the assistant's chair.

Reclaim

Chase & draft, then check

Let AI draft client emails, chase missing documents, and clear repetitive admin — then review before anything sends or files. The logistics are automatable; the accountability stays with you.

Verify

Every rule to source

AI states rules, rates, and deadlines with fabricated confidence — the tax version of the fake-citation problem. Confirm every provision against the statute or current official guidance before you rely on it.

Protect

Data behind the firewall

Never paste identifiable client financial data into a consumer AI tool. Keep it in sanctioned systems; de-identify or use placeholders before anything crosses. Confidentiality is an obligation, not a courtesy.

Sign

The human gate

Every AI output is a draft. Re-connect it to the real client, verify figures and rules to source, and sign. AI is decision-support; the filing, the advice, and the accountability are the accountant's.

The honest limits, one more time

Everything in this lesson depends on the same guardrails. Fabricated rules: AI will state a tax provision, rate, threshold, or deadline that is outdated or never existed, with total confidence — verify every rule against the primary source, exactly as the legal world learned to after the fake-citation cases. Unreliable arithmetic: a model may compute a liability or allowance wrongly while looking precise, so re-derive every figure. Client confidentiality: identifiable client financial data must never enter a consumer AI tool; keep it behind the firewall and work de-identified. Human sign-off: every output is a draft until a qualified human verifies and signs — AI is decision-support, never the source of record or the filer of record. Audit trail & skepticism: record what was checked and against what source, and treat fluent confidence as a reason to verify, not to relax. AI supports and calculates. The accountant verifies every rule and figure, protects the client's data, and signs. That is what lets a practice grow with AI instead of being undone by it.

What to carry forward

Compliance and client communication is where AI reclaims the most busy-season hours — and where an unverified rule or a leaked client figure can quietly undo a practice. Let AI chase, draft, and summarise; then verify every rule to a primary source, keep client data behind the firewall, and sign only what you've checked. AI is decision-support and never the source of record.
