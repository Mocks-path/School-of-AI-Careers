Industry Stop 1 · Research

The Research & Analysis Machine

An unfamiliar question, late on a Friday, a client waiting. For decades the honest answer was hours in the library or the database before she could even name the issues. Now she describes the facts in plain language and, in minutes, gets a map: issues spotted, doctrines in play, the shape of a memo she can build on.

It isn't that AI knows the law with authority — it doesn't, and it will cite cases that were never decided. It's research velocity: a fast first draft of her thinking — then she verifies every authority in a primary source.

Industry Stop 2 · Drafting & Review

Drafting & Review at Scale

A stack of forty vendor contracts or a data room full of due-diligence documents used to force a brutal trade-off: review fast and shallow, or review deeply and blow the deadline. Now AI reads each contract against a checklist, flags the missing indemnity clause, drafts a first redline, and surfaces the odd term buried on page ninety.

The non-negotiable rule the profession learned fast: the human stays in the loop on everything that goes out the door. AI drafts — the lawyer reviews, corrects, and signs their name to it.

Concept 1 of 4

Legal Research & Verified Analysis

Sound legal analysis has a checkable shape: a clearly framed question, the issues spotted, the governing authorities, and a conclusion that actually follows. AI is a tireless junior — describe your facts and it drafts an issue list, a doctrinal outline, and a first memo in seconds.

But hear this: AI suggests, the lawyer verifies. It hasn't read the current statute, doesn't know the case it cited was overruled last year, and will invent authorities that sound real. Research velocity is the gift — verification is your duty.

Concept 2 of 4

Drafting & Document Review

Every experienced lawyer knows: speed is worthless if it lowers the standard. A faster contract that misses the limitation-of-liability clause isn't faster — it's malpractice waiting to happen. AI can draft your clauses, correspondence, and first-pass redline in minutes instead of hours.

The catch the profession learned fast: AI confidently misses things and confidently invents others. It drops a defined term, softens an obligation, hallucinates a provision. Read every draft as if you'd written it.
Same clause · two levels of review
Speed without review — malpractice risk "AI redlined all forty contracts and I sent them out. Fast turnaround — the client was thrilled with the pace."
Speed + a trained eye (lawyer-verified) "AI drafted the redlines in an afternoon. Then I read each one closely — and caught the dropped indemnity term and a softened obligation AI had quietly reworded before anything went out."
Concept 3 of 4

Hallucinated Authorities & the Duty of Candor

Ask a general AI tool for supporting cases and it will often hand you a beautifully formatted list — correct-looking citations, plausible names, confident holdings — for decisions that were never decided. Lawyers have filed those fake cases in real courts, been sanctioned, named in orders, and reported to their bars.

Your duty of candor to the tribunal doesn't bend because a machine drafted the brief. The discipline is unforgiving: never cite what you haven't pulled and read in a primary source.
Career-ending mistake vs. the one habit

Treat every AI-supplied authority as unverified until you've confirmed it exists — and says what the AI claims.

Concept 4 of 4

Confidentiality, Privilege & Competence

Paste a client's name, their contract, their settlement figure, or the facts of their case into a random public AI tool, and you may hand privileged information to a company that stores it and trains on it — and you may have just waived the privilege that protects your client. That can end far worse than an embarrassing email.

Every other skill this week is optional. This one is the floor. Get this wrong and nothing else matters.

The Honest Slide

What AI Won't Fix

The slide most training sessions skip. Anyone who tells you a chatbot solves the justice crisis is not being straight with you.

Won't fix

Billing pressure, the crushing hours, the access-to-justice gap that leaves millions unable to afford a lawyer at all. AI does not touch what actually strains the profession.

What it does

It gives back hours — shrinking first-pass research, routine drafting, and reorganizing documents, so more time lands on the client, the judgment, and the reach of a small firm.

Stays yours forever

You verify every output — AI is confidently wrong — and you remain fully accountable to your client, the court, and your bar for everything under your name.

AI is a lever under your hours. It is never an excuse for your judgment.
