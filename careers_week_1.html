Chapter One · Sunday Night, Take One

Set Up the Workflow & the Ground Rules

Before Anaya asks AI for a single thing, she decides what AI is for — a drafting assistant, never a decision-maker — and draws two bright lines she will not cross: protect student data and verify every fact.

📖 The story so far Sunday, 8:10 p.m. Anaya's tea has gone cold beside a stack of unplanned lessons. A colleague swears AI "gives her Sundays back," but Anaya has read the horror stories too — a made-up citation here, a student's name pasted into a random website there. So before she types anything, she does the professional thing: she sets ground rules first, and uses AI to structure her work, not to replace her judgment.
🧭

AI drafts, I decide

AI produces a first draft in seconds; Anaya reads every line and owns the final call. The teacher's judgment is the point, not the bottleneck.

🔒

No real student data

No names, no roll numbers, no diagnoses, no photos into a general AI tool. She works with roles ("Student A") and needs, never identities.

🔍

Verify before I trust

Every fact, date, and quote gets checked against a real source. AI is confident even when it's wrong — so confidence is never her evidence.

Chapter Three · Marking, Reimagined

Build a Fair Rubric & Mark with Human-in-the-Loop

A fair assessment says up front what "good" looks like. AI can draft a clear rubric and even suggest feedback — but a grade is a judgment about a child, so Anaya keeps her hands firmly on the wheel: keep, fix, or discard every AI suggestion.

📖 The story so far Monday's exit tickets are in — 32 short answers on adding fractions. Old Anaya would have marked these on Sunday, in silence, at 11 p.m. New Anaya asks AI to draft a rubric first, then to suggest feedback comments from anonymised answers. AI is fast and mostly good — but "mostly" is exactly why she reads every one. AI drafts, Anaya decides.

Chapter Four · Behind the Firewall

Draft Parent Updates Behind a Privacy Firewall

Parent notes are where AI saves the most time — and where a careless paste can leak a child's private data. Anaya's trick: draft with placeholders, let AI shape the tone, then fill in real names herself, offline, at the very end.

📖 The story so far Thursday evening. Report notes for 32 families are due. The temptation is huge: paste the whole class spreadsheet into AI and say "write me 32 notes." Anaya doesn't. She builds a privacy firewall — AI never sees a real name or score. It writes the warm, clear scaffolding; she pours the real child into it afterward. Same time saved, zero data leaked.
🚫 The tempting shortcut (never do this)

"Write a report for Aarav Sharma, roll 14, who scored 54% and has ADHD, and copy this to the parent."

A real name, a real score, a health note — pasted into a general tool whose data handling you don't control. That's a child's private record leaving the building.

✅ The privacy firewall (do this)

"Draft a warm, specific parent note for [STUDENT] who is making steady progress in fractions and would benefit from more reading-aloud at home. Leave name and details as placeholders."

AI writes the scaffolding from needs, not identities. Anaya fills [STUDENT] in herself, offline. Fast and safe.

✉️ Same note, three tones — you direct the voice

AI can write in whatever register a family needs. Anaya picks the tone; the placeholders stay until she fills them in privately. Try each.

Lesson 1

AI Workflow & Ground Rules

AI drafts, you decide. Two bright lines: never feed real student data to general tools, and verify every fact — confidence isn't evidence. Chapter 1.

Lesson 2

Differentiated, Standards-Aligned Planning

One standard, many doors in. AI drafts three readiness tiers with the same goal; real differentiation adds scaffolds, not just less work. Chapter 2.

Lesson 3

Fair Assessment & Human-in-the-Loop

A rubric before a grade. AI drafts feedback from anonymised work; you keep, fix, or discard every line, because a grade is a judgment about a child. Chapter 3.

Lesson 4

Parent Notes Behind a Privacy Firewall

Draft with placeholders and needs, not names and scores. AI shapes tone; you fill in the real child offline. Biggest time-saver, zero leaks. Chapter 4.
