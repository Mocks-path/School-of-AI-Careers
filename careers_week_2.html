Chapter One · The Engagement Letter Is Signed

Set Up the AI Workflow & the Ground Rules

Before Maya asks AI for a single thing, she decides what AI is for — a drafting assistant, never a decision-maker or a source of law — and draws two bright lines she will not cross: protect confidentiality & privilege and verify every authority and fact.

📖 The story so far Monday, 9:05 a.m. A new client — a small supplier entering a distribution deal — has just signed Maya's engagement letter. The clock is running and the budget is tight. A friend at a bigger firm swears AI "does half her drafting now," but Maya has read the news too: a lawyer sanctioned for filing fake AI-invented cases, a firm scolded for pasting client secrets into a public chatbot. So before she types anything, she does the professional thing: she sets ground rules first, and uses AI to structure her work, not to replace her judgment or her duty to the client.
⚖️

AI drafts, I decide

AI produces a first draft in seconds; Maya reads every line and owns the legal advice. The lawyer's judgment is the point, not the bottleneck — and it's her name on the file.

🔒

No client confidences in public tools

No names, deal terms, or privileged facts into a general AI tool whose data handling she can't control. She works with placeholders and abstractions, never real identities.

🔍

Verify every authority

Every case, statute, quote, and date gets checked against a real legal source. AI is confident even when it invents authorities — so confidence is never her evidence.

Chapter Two · The Question Behind the Deal

Research a Legal Question — and Verify Every Authority

AI can point Maya toward issues and frame a research question in minutes — but it can also fabricate cases that sound perfectly real. So she treats every citation as unverified until she reads it in a genuine source. This chapter's mini-game: catch the hallucinated authority before it reaches a client.

📖 The story so far Monday, 11:20 a.m. The client's worry: if the distributor leaks their supplier list, can they actually enforce the confidentiality clause and claim damages? Maya asks AI to frame the research question and list the issues — reasonable use of a fast assistant. Then it volunteers four supporting "authorities," neatly formatted. Old habits would copy them straight into a memo. New Maya does the one thing that separates a professional from a headline: she verifies every single one against a real database before she trusts a word. Chapter Three · Red Pen on the Contract

Draft & Review the NDA with a Playbook

AI can draft a clean NDA and even flag risky clauses — but a contract term is a commitment your client lives with, so Maya reviews it against a playbook (her standard positions) and keeps her hands firmly on the wheel: keep, fix, or reject every AI suggestion. The trap this chapter teaches: AI's most dangerous mistakes are the ones it misses.

📖 The story so far Tuesday, 2:00 p.m. The distributor's lawyers send over their NDA draft. Maya asks AI to summarise it and flag anything unusual — a genuinely useful first pass. AI catches an over-broad non-compete and a missing return-of-materials clause. Good. But it stays silent on a perpetual, worldwide confidentiality obligation buried in clause 7 — a term that would bind her client forever. AI drafts and flags; Maya reviews against her playbook and catches what it missed.
📕 Maya's NDA Playbook · her standard positions (the human layer AI can't replace)
Clause
Maya's standard position
Confidentiality term
Time-limited (e.g. 3–5 years post-termination), not perpetual. Perpetual + worldwide is a red flag to fix.
Definition of "Confidential Information"
Specific, with standard carve-outs (public domain, independently developed, lawfully received). Reject "everything, forever."
Remedies & damages
Reasonable and mutual. Be wary of one-sided liquidated-damages or "automatic punitive" language.
Return / destruction of materials
Must be present. If missing, add it — AI often forgets this one.
💡 Why a playbook? A playbook is Maya's memory of what "acceptable" looks like, written down. AI reviews against the words on the page; the playbook lets Maya review against her client's interests. AI drafted a summary; the playbook is what turns a summary into a decision.
🖊️ Help Maya decide: Keep · Fix · Reject

AI reviewed three clauses (and Maya spotted a fourth thing it missed). Human-in-the-loop means you don't rubber-stamp — judge each clause against the playbook. Decide for all three to see Maya's verdict.

Clause 4 · Definition of Confidential Information
"'Confidential Information' means all information disclosed by either party, excluding information that is public, independently developed, or lawfully received from a third party."
AI's note: "Standard mutual definition with the usual carve-outs. Looks fine." Against the playbook: specific, with proper exceptions.
Clause 7 · Duration (the one AI stayed silent on)
"The obligations of confidentiality shall continue in perpetuity and apply worldwide, without limitation of time."
AI's note: (none — AI did not flag this). Against the playbook: perpetual + worldwide is exactly the red flag. Maya caught it, not AI.
Clause 11 · Remedies
"Any breach, however minor, shall automatically entitle the Disclosing Party to punitive damages of a fixed sum, payable on demand, regardless of actual loss."
AI's note: "Flagged — remedies clause is one-sided and unusually harsh." Against the playbook: reject "automatic punitive, regardless of loss" language.

Chapter Four · Behind the Confidentiality Firewall

Write a Plain-Language Client Update — Behind a Firewall

Client updates are where AI saves the most non-billable time — and where a careless paste can breach confidentiality or waive privilege. Maya's trick: draft with placeholders, let AI shape the plain-language tone, then fill in real names and deal terms herself, offline, at the very end.

📖 The story so far Wednesday, 4:30 p.m. The client needs an update — in plain English, not legalese. The temptation is huge: paste the full memo, the client's name, and the deal figures into AI and say "turn this into a friendly email." Maya doesn't. She builds a confidentiality firewall — AI never sees a real name, number, or privileged fact. It writes the warm, clear scaffolding; she pours the real matter into it afterward. Same non-billable time saved, zero confidence breached.
🚫 The tempting shortcut (never do this)

"Rewrite this for my client Acme Supplies Ltd: their distributor Novacorp wants a perpetual NDA on the ₹4.2 crore supply deal, and our privileged advice is to walk away."

Real party names, deal value, and privileged strategy — pasted into a general tool whose data handling you don't control. That's a client confidence (and possibly privilege) leaving the building.

✅ The confidentiality firewall (do this)

"Draft a warm, plain-language client update for [CLIENT]. Explain that the other side's confidentiality term is too broad and we've proposed a time limit. Keep names, figures, and strategy as placeholders."

AI writes the scaffolding from issues, not identities. Maya fills [CLIENT] and the specifics in herself, offline. Fast and safe.
