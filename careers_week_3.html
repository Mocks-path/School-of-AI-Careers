Chapter One · The Waiting Room Is Full

Set Up the AI Workflow & the Safety Ground Rules

Before Dr. Nadia asks AI for a single thing, she decides what AI is for — decision support, never a diagnosis or a decision-maker — and draws two bright lines she will not cross: protect patient privacy and verify everything.

📖 The story so far Monday, 8:40 a.m. The waiting room is full, the inbox has forty messages, and Dr. Nadia already knows how tonight ends: dinner reheated, charting until 11. A colleague swears an AI scribe "gave her an hour back a day," but Nadia has read the news too: a chatbot inventing a drug dose, a clinic that pasted a patient's whole history into a consumer app. So before she types anything, she does the professional thing: she sets safety ground rules first, and uses AI to lighten her workload — not to replace her clinical judgment or her duty to the patient.
🩺

AI suggests, I decide

AI drafts and summarises in seconds; Dr. Nadia reads every line and owns the clinical call. It is decision-support, never the diagnosis — and it's her name and licence on the chart.

🔒

No patient data in consumer tools

No names, dates of birth, or identifiable history into a general AI app whose data handling she can't control. She works with placeholders and de-identified facts, never a real identity.

🔍

Verify every fact & dose

Every reference, guideline, and dose gets checked against a real clinical source. AI sounds confident even when it invents a study or a number — so confidence is never her evidence.

Chapter Two · The Question Behind the Visit

Check the Evidence — and Verify Every Fact & Dose

AI can help Dr. Nadia frame a clinical question and surface issues in minutes — but it can also fabricate studies that sound perfectly real and quote an outdated recommendation with total confidence. So she treats every reference and number as unverified until she reads it in a genuine source. This chapter's mini-game: catch the fabricated study before it reaches a patient.

📖 The story so far Monday, 11:20 a.m. A patient asks a fair question: for someone like them, does one common preventive approach actually help, and at what dose? Nadia asks AI to frame the question and list the evidence to look at — a reasonable use of a fast assistant. It volunteers four supporting "references," neatly formatted, plus a specific dose. Old habits would repeat them straight to the patient. New Nadia does the one thing that separates a careful clinician from a headline: she verifies every reference and every number against a real, current guideline before she trusts a word. Below, three angles on how she structures that check.

Chapter Three · Red Pen on the Chart

Review the AI-Drafted Note — Before You Attest

An AI scribe can turn a visit into a clean draft note in seconds — but the note is a legal medical record your patient's future care rests on, so Dr. Nadia reviews it against what actually happened and keeps her hands firmly on the wheel: keep, fix, or reject every line before she signs. The trap this chapter teaches: AI's most dangerous mistakes are the ones it invents — a finding that never happened, or the wrong side of the body.

📖 The story so far Tuesday, 2:00 p.m. The AI scribe listened to the visit and produced a tidy draft note — genuinely a time-saver. Most of it is accurate. But two lines are wrong in ways that matter: it wrote a normal lung exam Dr. Nadia never actually performed or dictated (a hallucinated finding), and it recorded knee pain on the wrong side — "left" when the patient pointed to the right. AI drafts; Nadia reviews against reality and catches what it got wrong — before she attests.
📕 Dr. Nadia's Note-Review Checklist · what she checks before signing (the human layer AI can't replace)
What to check
Dr. Nadia's standard
Findings actually happened
Every exam finding must reflect what she truly examined and said. A "normal" exam she never did is a fabrication to delete — not a convenience to keep.
Laterality & specifics
Left vs. right, dose, frequency, date. AI transcribes confidently but can flip a side or a number. Fix against what actually occurred.
Assessment & plan are hers
The diagnosis and plan must be her clinical judgement, not AI's guess. Reasonable draft language is fine; the medical decision is hers to own.
Attestation = accountability
Signing the note means "this is accurate and it's my care." She never attests to a line she hasn't verified. If unsure, fix or remove it.

AI drafts against the words it heard; the checklist lets her review against what truly happened and what the patient needs. Chapter Four · Behind the Privacy Firewall

Write a Plain-Language After-Visit Summary — Behind a Firewall

After-visit summaries are where AI saves the most evening time — and where a careless paste can expose protected health information (PHI). Dr. Nadia's trick: draft with placeholders and de-identified facts, let AI shape the plain-language tone, then fill in the real name and details herself, offline, inside her secure record at the very end.

📖 The story so far Wednesday, 4:30 p.m. The patient needs a summary — in plain language, not clinician-speak. The temptation is huge: paste the whole chart, the name, the date of birth, and the medications into a consumer AI app and say "turn this into a friendly handout." Dr. Nadia doesn't. She builds a privacy firewall — AI never sees a real name, date, or identifiable detail. It writes the warm, clear scaffolding; she pours the real patient information into it afterward, inside her secure system. Same evening time saved, zero PHI exposed.
🚫 The tempting shortcut (never do this)

"Rewrite this for my patient Anand Verma, DOB 12 March 1971, at 14 Rosewood Lane: he has a right knee sprain, I started him on a specific medication and dose, and his phone number is below."

Real name, date of birth, address, phone, and specifics — pasted into a general consumer tool whose data handling you don't control. That's protected health information leaving your secure system.

✅ The privacy firewall (do this)

"Draft a warm, plain-language after-visit summary for [PATIENT] about a minor knee injury. Explain rest and gentle movement, when to seek care, and next follow-up. Keep all names, dates, and specifics as placeholders."

AI writes the scaffolding from a condition, not an identity. Dr. Nadia fills [PATIENT] and the specifics in herself, inside her secure record. Fast and safe.

🖤 The redaction beat — scrub it before it goes near AI

Lesson 1

AI Workflow & Safety Ground Rules

AI suggests, you verify and decide. Two bright lines: never put patient data in consumer tools, and AI must not diagnose or triage the record — it's decision-support, not a decision-maker. Chapter 1.

Lesson 2

Checking the Evidence & Verifying

AI frames the question and maps the evidence fast — but it can fabricate studies and quote outdated doses. Read every reference and confirm every number in a current source. Chapter 2.

Lesson 3

Reviewing the AI-Drafted Note

A scribe drafts; you review before you attest. Keep, fix, or reject every line — and catch what AI invented (a finding that never happened) or flipped (the wrong side). Chapter 3.

Lesson 4

Patient Summaries Behind a Firewall

Draft with placeholders and a condition, not names and dates. AI shapes plain-language tone; you fill in the real details inside your secure record. Biggest time-saver, zero PHI exposed. Chapter 4.
