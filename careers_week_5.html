Chapter One · The Close That Won't Wait

Set Up the AI Workflow & the Ground Rules

Before Priya asks AI for a single figure, she decides what AI is for — decision support and drafting, never the source of record — and draws the bright lines she will not cross: protect client confidentiality, never trust AI arithmetic, and verify every figure to its source before it touches the ledger.

📖 The story so far Monday, 8:40 a.m. of close week. The books have to be shut by Friday, the board wants variance commentary by Thursday, and a batch of transactions is sitting un-categorised. A peer swears an AI tool "closed her month in half the time," but Priya has read the news too: a chatbot that confidently added a column wrong, a model that invented a tax provision that doesn't exist, a firm that pasted a whole client trial balance into a consumer app. So before Priya types anything, she does the professional thing: she sets confidentiality and verification ground rules first, and uses AI to lighten the workload — never to replace the figures she signs or her duty to keep client data safe.
🖊️

AI supports, I verify & sign

AI extracts, categorises, drafts, and calculates in seconds; Priya reconciles every figure to source and owns the numbers. It is decision-support, never the source of record — her signature is on the close, and the books have to be right.

🔒

No client financials in consumer tools

No client names, account numbers, or identifiable financial data into a general AI app whose data handling she can't control. She works with de-identified figures and placeholders, never a client's confidential books.

🧮

Never trust AI arithmetic

AI is a language model, not a calculator — it can state a total that looks right and is simply wrong. Every sum, subtotal, and percentage is recomputed and tied back to the underlying detail before it counts.

🔍

Verify every figure & rule to source

Every amount, tax code, and cited regulation gets checked against the actual document, ledger, or primary source. AI sounds confident even when it's inventing a number or a provision that doesn't exist.

Chapter Two · From Batch to Balanced

Extract & Categorise the Batch — then Reconcile to Source

AI can help Priya extract and categorise a messy transaction batch in minutes — but it can also state a total that's simply wrong, mis-read an amount off a document, and apply the wrong tax code. So Priya treats every AI figure as unverified until she has recomputed it and tied it back to source. This chapter's mini-game: catch the hallucinated total, the mis-read amount, and the wrong tax code before they reach the ledger.

📖 The story so far Monday, 11:20 a.m. The batch is a pile of receipts, invoices, and bank lines. Priya asks AI to extract each transaction, categorise it, and total the batch — a reasonable use of a fast assistant. It returns a tidy, categorised table with a confident grand total at the bottom. Old habits would post it straight to the ledger. New Priya does the one thing that separates a careful accountant from a restatement: she recomputes every total herself and ties every amount and tax code back to the source document before trusting a single figure. Below, three angles on how she structures that check.

Chapter Three · Numbers That Tell a Story

Draft Variance Commentary & the Board Report — then Verify Every Claim

Variance commentary — explaining why each line moved against budget and last period — is where a good accountant turns numbers into insight. AI can draft the narrative and the board summary in seconds — but Priya keeps her hands on the wheel: accept, fix, or investigate every line before it goes to the board. The traps this chapter teaches: AI will confidently state a growth % it never calculated, carry a wrong subtotal, and assert an unsupported causal claim about why a number moved.

📖 The story so far Tuesday, 2:00 p.m. The books are closed and reconciled. Priya asks AI to turn the numbers into variance commentary and a board-ready summary — genuinely a time-saver. Most of it is accurate and well-phrased. But three lines are wrong in ways that matter: AI wrote that "revenue grew 27% year-on-year" (a percentage that doesn't match the actual figures), carried a "total operating expenses" subtotal that's off, and asserted that "the marketing campaign drove the sales increase" — a causal claim the data never supports. AI drafts; Priya checks every figure against the reconciled ledger and every claim against the evidence, and catches the fabricated growth %, the wrong subtotal, and the unsupported causal story — before it reaches the board.
📕 Priya's Report-Review Checklist · what she checks before a line goes to the board (the human layer AI can't replace)
What to check
Priya's standard
Every % recomputed
Every growth rate, margin, and variance percentage is recalculated from the reconciled figures — never taken from AI's prose. A model will state a clean-looking "+27%" that the actual numbers don't produce. If it isn't recomputed, it doesn't ship.
Every subtotal ties out
Each subtotal and total in the narrative must equal the sum of its parts in the reconciled ledger. AI can carry a total forward that no longer adds up. A subtotal that doesn't tie is a number to fix, not a sentence to keep.
Correlation ≠ causation
"X drove Y" is a claim about why, and the ledger only shows what. AI loves a tidy causal story the data can't support. Priya softens it to what's evidenced ("revenue rose; marketing spend also rose") or investigates before asserting cause.
Same source, every time
Every figure in the report traces to the one reconciled, signed set of books — the source of record. AI's draft is a retelling; the ledger is the truth. Priya never lets a polished sentence quietly replace the reconciled number.
💡 Why review the narrative? A board report is Priya's professional word on what the numbers mean. AI drafts against the words it was given; the reconciled ledger is what Priya checks against. AI produced a summary; her review is what turns a fluent draft into a report she can put her name behind — with every figure right and every claim supported.

AI's draft: a confident cause-and-effect story. Against the evidence: the ledger shows sales rose and marketing spend rose — it does not show one caused the other. Several things changed at once. This is a claim to investigate or soften to what's supported, not a fact to assert to the board.

Chapter Four · Behind the Confidentiality Firewall

Research the Rule to Source, Redact the Client Data & Send a Clear Update

Compliance research and the client communication are where AI saves the most time — and where a fabricated regulation can end up in a filing, and a careless paste can expose confidential client financials. Priya's trick: research the rule with AI but verify it against the primary source (catching a fabricated provision), draft the client update with placeholders and de-identified figures behind a confidentiality firewall, then fill in the real client and numbers herself, inside her secure system.

📖 The story so far Wednesday, 4:30 p.m. A compliance question came up during the close, and the client needs a clear update — accurately and promptly. Priya asks AI to explain the relevant rule. It answers fluently and cites a specific provision by number. The temptation is huge: quote it straight into the file, then paste the whole client trial balance into a consumer AI app and say "summarise this for the client." Priya doesn't. She verifies the cited rule against the primary source — and finds AI invented a provision that doesn't exist. She builds a confidentiality firewall: AI never sees a real client name, account number, or confidential figure. It drafts a clear update scaffold; Priya pours the real client information in afterward, inside her secure system, and checks every number before it sends. Same time saved, zero client data exposed, no fabricated rule filed.

🚫 The tempting shortcut (never do this)

"Summarise this for the client — here's the full trial balance for Nadia Rahman t/a Rosewood Bakery (client acct 44192, TIN 88-2210), with every account balance, the owner's drawings, and the bank details pasted below — and just tell them what they owe."

Real client name, account number, tax ID, full confidential financials, and bank details — pasted into a general consumer tool whose data handling you don't control, plus a tax figure taken on trust with no verification. That's confidential client data leaving your secure system, and a number sent without being checked.

✅ The confidentiality firewall (do this)

"Draft a clear, friendly month-end update for [CLIENT] explaining that their books are closed and reconciled, one figure moved notably versus budget, and a filing is due soon. Keep all names, account numbers, and specific amounts as placeholders."

AI writes the scaffolding from a status, not an identity. Priya fills [CLIENT] and the verified figures in herself, inside the secure system, and checks every number before it sends. Fast, clear, and confidential.

Week 5 · You are here

The meta-skill you actually learned

  1. Direct AI — give it a clear role and constraints; delegate the drafting, structuring, and calculating drudgery.
  2. Verify everything — every fact, figure, citation, and rule checked against the real source. AI is confident even when it's wrong.
  3. Apply judgment — the fairness call, the clinical call, the professional call stays yours. AI proposes; you decide.
  4. Protect people — student, client, patient, candidate, or account: their confidential data never enters a consumer tool.
  5. Own the decision — your name is on the outcome. AI supports; you sign.

Teachers, lawyers, doctors, recruiters, accountants — the tools differ, the traps rhyme, and the answer is always the same: let AI carry the drudgery so more of you goes into the work that needs a human. That's not a trick for one job. It's a way of working you can take into any of them. Congratulations — you've completed School of AI Careers. 🎉
