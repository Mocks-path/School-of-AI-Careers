Planning & Designing Instruction — a vague prompt gets a vague plan

Three hands-on activities. Build a strong lesson brief by picking the elements that actually matter, match differentiation supports to real learners, then judge AI-generated output with keep, fix, or discard — including spotting a hallucinated standard before it reaches your class.

Match the support to the learner

Differentiation is not "easier" and "harder" — it is the right support for a specific need. AI can draft a scaffold, a core task, an extension, or a language-support version in seconds; your job is knowing which learner needs which. Drag each AI-drafted support to the learner it fits. Judge the AI output — keep, fix, or discard?

AI just returned a lesson plan. Some of it is genuinely useful, some needs a quick edit, and one line is quietly dangerous — a hallucinated standard that sounds official but does not exist. Read each piece of output and decide: keep it as-is, fix it before use, or discard it entirely. This is "AI drafts, the teacher decides" in action.

Three checks to run on every AI-generated plan

Activity 3 showed the traps in one plan. Here they are as a permanent checklist. A polished draft can still be wrong, skewed, or unsafe — and a plan looks finished precisely when it is most tempting to skip the check. Run these three every time before a plan reaches your class.

Verify Hallucination

AI invents standards, objectives, and "facts" that read as official but do not exist — exactly the trap in Activity 3. It has no live curriculum access and fills gaps with confident fiction.

Check: cross-reference every cited standard and factual claim against the real curriculum document. Unverified means discard, not keep.

Balance Bias

AI reaches for default names, examples, and contexts that can quietly exclude the children in front of you — and it treats one cultural frame as the neutral one without telling you.

Check: read the plan for who is represented and who is missing. Swap in names, examples, and contexts that reflect your actual class.

Protect Privacy

It is tempting to paste a struggling pupil's real details to get a "personalised" plan. That data can be stored and used to train the model — a breach dressed up as convenience.

Check: brief in generic terms only. Match the plan to the real child yourself, adding the personal context during your own edit.
💡 Verify, balance, protect. A strong brief gets you a strong draft; these three checks turn a strong draft into a safe lesson. The AI generates fast — you decide what is true, fair, and safe enough to teach.
