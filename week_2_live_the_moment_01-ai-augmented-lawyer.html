The AI-Augmented Lawyer — AI drafts, the lawyer verifies and decides

Three hands-on activities. Sort real legal tasks into what AI can safely draft, what only a lawyer can judge, and what must never touch a public tool. Then lock in the one pattern that keeps AI useful and honest — including how to catch a fabricated citation — and reveal where AI helps across your five core legal workflows.

Progress
1

Sort the task — draft it, judge it, or never touch it?

Not every legal task belongs near an AI tool. The useful question is not "can AI do this?" but "what is at stake if it gets this wrong — a wasted edit, a client's outcome, or a breach of privilege?" Three buckets, three risk levels. Study the cards, then drag each task where it belongs. Guess freely — wrong pills carry a hover hint with the answer.

AI

Great for AI — draft

Low-stakes, high-volume first drafts you will read and verify anyway: routine clauses, summaries, plain-language explanations, timelines. AI beats the blank page; your judgment finishes the job.

+ Clears the slow "blank page" minutes + Offers structure and variety to refine − Always verify law, facts, and citations − A draft is never filed or sent as-is
YOU

Lawyer-only — judgment

Calls that define a client's rights and outcomes: legal advice, strategy, whether to settle, what a court will accept. AI can inform, never decide — and never bears the duty owed to the client.

+ You hold the duty and the accountability + You read the law in context AI cannot see − AI has no professional responsibility − Outsourcing judgment risks the client
NO

Never — confidential in public tools

Anything that would paste privileged or client-identifying material into a public tool: matter facts, party names, settlement figures, strategy, documents under a duty of confidentiality.

+ Protects privilege and confidentiality + Keeps you inside your professional duties − A single paste can waive privilege − "I'll anonymise it" is often not enough
💡 The rule of thumb: if a mistake would only cost you a minute of editing, AI can draft it. If a mistake would harm a client's rights or outcome, you judge it. If a mistake would expose privileged or confidential matter to a public tool, it never goes in.

The honest part — what AI gets wrong, and how you guard against it

Being augmented does not mean being uncritical. Three failure modes show up in every legal AI tool, no matter how fluent. Knowing them — and the simple guard for each — is what separates a professional using AI from someone who trusted a chatbot and got sanctioned.

Hallucinated authority Invents law

AI will state made-up case names, fake citations, non-existent statutes, and quotations that were never written — confidently, in fluent legal prose. Lawyers have been sanctioned for filing briefs built on cases that do not exist.

Guard: verify every case, statute, and quotation in an authoritative primary source before you rely on it. If you cannot pull it up and read it, it does not exist.

Bias Skews outcomes

Trained on human text, AI can reproduce bias — in how it frames parties, which arguments it treats as "standard", and assumptions baked into risk or sentencing-style outputs. It will not flag this for you.

Guard: read every draft for whose interests it centres and what it assumes. Apply your own professional judgment to arguments, framing, and any recommendation about people.

Confidentiality & privilege Leaks data

Text pasted into a public tool can be stored and used to train future models. Client facts, party names, or strategy entered "just to get help" can breach confidentiality and risk waiving privilege.

Guard: never enter privileged or client-identifying material into a public tool. Work in generic, hypothetical terms; keep the matter detail out and add it back in your own edit.
💡 Put together, these are one habit: treat AI output as a confident junior's first draft — fast and helpful, never trusted unread. You verify every authority against a primary source, you check the framing and assumptions, and you keep privileged matter out of public tools. That is the whole discipline of the augmented lawyer.

Lesson 1 complete — what you now carry

›Sort by stakes, not by capability. Ask what a mistake would cost — an edit, a client's outcome, or a breach of privilege — before you ask whether AI can do the task.
›AI drafts; the lawyer verifies and decides. The tool proposes; you own the advice, the strategy, and the duty to the client and the court.
›Confidentiality is a hard line. Privileged and client-identifying material never goes into a public tool — convenience never wins that trade.
›Be honest about limits. AI hallucinates authorities, carries bias, and cannot see your matter — verification against primary sources is part of the workflow, not an extra.
