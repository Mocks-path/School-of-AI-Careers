Client Communication, Matter Management & Growth — protect privilege, then let AI polish

Three hands-on activities on the client-facing side of practice. First, build a confidentiality firewall: redact the privileged and identifying details before any prompt could reach a public AI tool. Then tune a dense legal message into plain, client-appropriate language that stays accurate. Finally, assemble the whole week — research, draft, review, communicate — into one matter that a human owns end to end.

“

The rule that protects your client and your licence: anything you type into a public AI tool may be stored and used to train it — and confidential client facts pasted into one can put privilege at risk. So the safe workflow is redact first, draft second, review always. AI can turn a dense legal update into something a client actually understands — but only if you never hand it an identity, and never send its words unread.

Plain language, still accurate

AI is genuinely good at turning dense legalese into something a client can read — but it can also flatten a message until it's wrong, or over-promise in the name of sounding friendly. For each dense legal message, all four rewrites are more readable than the original. Pick the one that's plain, client-appropriate, and still legally accurate — the version you'd actually be willing to sign.

Matter complete.

You just ran a complete AI-assisted legal matter — and at every single stage, a human verified and stayed accountable. That's the whole philosophy of this course in one screen: AI drafts, the lawyer verifies and decides.

  • ✓ Research — AI finds leads, you pull every authority and confirm nothing was invented.
  • ✓ Draft — AI drafts, you fit it to the matter and verify every term.
  • ✓ Review — you accept, override, and catch the miss AI stayed silent on.
  • ✓ Communicate — privilege redacted first, plain but accurate, every word read before it's sent.

Where this frees your time — and grows you

"Professional growth" isn't a buzzword here. The hours AI hands back on drafting and admin are hours you can spend on the parts of lawyering only a human can do — client trust, judgement, strategy — and on getting better at your craft. Two honest framings to take with you.

+

Where AI can genuinely lighten the load

High-volume, no-identity, first-draft work — draft it fast, then verify.

  • First drafts of client updates and routine correspondence (you add the specifics)
  • Turning a dense legal analysis into a plain-language explainer to edit
  • Summarising long documents so you know where to read closely
  • Drafting matter timelines and status notes from your own bullet points
–

Where the lawyer is the point

The relationship, the judgement, and the duty of care never delegate.

  • The advice itself, and the decision on what risk a client accepts
  • A difficult conversation with a worried or angry client
  • Anything involving unredacted client facts or privileged assessments
  • Signing your name to advice or a message you haven't actually read
