Week 4 · AI for Recruiters

Sourcing & Screening with AI — fair criteria first, AI as support, the recruiter decides

Three hands-on activities. Build inclusive, defensible criteria and strip exclusionary language from a job description. Then judge a set of AI screening outputs — flag which need human review, which reflect proxy bias, and which credit a candidate with a qualification they never had. Finally, put the sourcing-and-screening workflow in the order that keeps hiring fair, with human review before anyone is shortlisted or turned away.

Progress
1

Build inclusive criteria — must-have vs nice-to-have, and cut the exclusionary language

Fair screening starts long before any AI tool runs. If your criteria are bloated with wish-list requirements and your job description quietly signals who "belongs," AI will simply screen faster in a biased way. First, sort each criterion into a genuine must-have or a nice-to-have. Then read the draft job description and click the phrases that are exclusionary, gendered, or ageist — the ones a fair recruiter would remove.

✓

Must-have

Genuinely essential to do the job — the small set of capabilities without which no one could succeed in the role. Keep this list short and job-related; every extra "must" narrows your pool and risks screening out capable people.

+

Nice-to-have

Helpful but not essential — things a strong hire could learn on the job or bring as a bonus. Treating these as musts is one of the most common ways criteria quietly exclude great candidates from non-traditional paths.

✕

Exclusionary language

Phrases that signal a preferred age, gender, or background rather than a real requirement — "young and energetic," "digital native," "he'll thrive," "recent graduate." These create adverse impact and belong nowhere in a fair JD.

2

Shortlist or review? — reading AI screening outputs honestly

AI can summarise and rank applicants in seconds — but a fluent output is not a fair one. For each screening result, decide the right next move. Some need a closer human look, some reflect proxy bias or adverse impact, and one credits a candidate with a qualification they never listed. In none of them is fully-automated rejection the answer. Pick an option for instant feedback. Answer all four to see your score.

3

Put the sourcing & screening workflow in order

A fair, AI-supported hiring funnel follows a deliberate order — criteria and inclusive language come first, AI screens only as support, and a human reviews before anyone is shortlisted. Drag the five steps into the sequence that keeps the process fair and defensible, then check your ordering.

Drag to reorder. Step 1 at the top, step 5 at the bottom.

💡 The order is the safeguard: define fair criteria and write an inclusive JD before you source, let AI screen only as support, and keep a human review between the AI output and any shortlist or rejection. Skip a step — especially the human review — and speed turns into unfair, unaccountable filtering.

✓

Lesson 2 complete — what you now carry

›Fairness starts with the criteria. Keep must-haves short and job-related; demoting wish-list items to nice-to-haves widens your pool and reduces adverse impact before any tool runs.
›Cut exclusionary language. Gendered, ageist, or "culture-fit" phrasing signals who belongs and quietly narrows applicants. Describe the work, never the person.
›AI screens as support, never as the decision. Read every ranking for proxy bias and hallucinated qualifications, verify claims against the real resume, and keep a human review before anyone is shortlisted or rejected.
›Order is a safeguard. Define criteria → inclusive JD → source → screen as support → shortlist with human review. No applicant is ever auto-rejected without a human in the loop.
