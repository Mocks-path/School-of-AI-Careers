Week 4 · AI for Recruiters

Assessment, Decision & Hiring Operations — protect the candidate, then decide on evidence

This is where a req becomes a hire — or an unfair rejection. First you'll build a candidate-privacy firewall: strip every identifying detail before a prompt could reach a consumer AI tool. Then you'll make the call — the fair, evidence-based decision and the kind, timely, human-reviewed message to the candidate — dodging the trap of auto-rejection and opaque AI scoring. Finally you'll assemble the whole week into one human-owned hiring workflow and fill the req — fairly.

Progress
“

The rule that protects your candidate and your process: anything you type into a consumer AI tool may be stored and used to train it — so candidate personal data must never go in. And no candidate should ever be rejected by a score no human reviewed. The safe workflow is de-identify first, decide on evidence, review as a human always. AI can help you draft a fair rationale or a warm rejection — but only if you never hand it an identity, never let an opaque score make the call, and never send its words unread. AI supports; the recruiter verifies and decides.

The decision — fair, evidence-based, human-reviewed

Now you make the call. A fair hiring decision rests on the evidence from a structured process — not on an opaque AI score, and never on an auto-rejection no human looked at. And however the decision lands, the candidate deserves a kind, timely message a person actually reviewed. For each situation, pick the response that is fair, evidence-based, and human-owned. One option in each set is a trap: it lets a score or an automation decide for you.

  • ✓ Fair criteria & inclusive JD — job-related requirements, human-reviewed for exclusionary language.

Where this frees your time — and where it must not

The hours AI hands back on drafting, scheduling, and summarising are hours you can spend on the parts of recruiting only a person can do — the judgement, the fairness, the candidate experience. But some things must never be handed to a score. Two honest framings to take with you.

+

Where AI can genuinely lighten the load

High-volume, de-identified, first-draft work — draft it fast, then verify.

  • Drafting a rejection or offer message for you to review, warm and time it
  • Summarising a structured scorecard so you can write a clear rationale
  • Drafting inclusive JD language and interview questions you standardise
  • Scheduling logistics and routine candidate updates you approve before sending
–

Where the recruiter is the point

The decision, the fairness, and the candidate's dignity never delegate.

  • The hire / no-hire decision — made on evidence, never on an opaque score
  • Auto-rejecting anyone without a human reviewing the call
  • Anything involving identifiable candidate data in a consumer tool you don't control
  • Putting your name to a decision or message you haven't actually read
