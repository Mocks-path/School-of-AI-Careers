Analysis, Reporting & Advisory — let AI draft the story, you verify every number

Clean books are only the start. The value you add is turning numbers into insight a client can act on. AI can pull a variance, draft the commentary, and structure the advice in seconds — but it also hallucinates growth rates, botches arithmetic, and invents tidy causal stories that the data never supported. In this lesson you'll sit in the reviewer's chair: order the analysis workflow, catch a fabricated figure and an unsupported claim in an AI-drafted commentary, and learn to tell a verified, defensible statement from confident false precision. The report goes out under your name — so it goes out verified.

Progress
“

The rule that keeps your analysis defensible: AI is a fast, confident drafter that cannot be trusted with a number or a cause. It does arithmetic unreliably, invents growth percentages that sound plausible, and manufactures neat causal explanations the data never proves. So every figure ties back to the source ledger, every percentage is recomputed, and every "because" is either evidenced or downgraded to "may be associated with." AI supports and calculates a draft; the accountant verifies the figures, tests the claims, and signs.

Verified & defensible — or false precision?

The final skill of analysis is knowing what you can actually stand behind. AI-generated reporting language tends toward two failure modes: false precision (a specific-sounding number or forecast with no verified basis) and spurious causation (a confident "because" the data cannot prove). For each statement, decide whether it's verified & defensible or false precision / spurious causation — then pick the better, honestly caveated version to send.

Classify each statement, then choose the version you'd actually put in front of a client.

Final judgement call: your AI draft ends the report with a forward-looking line. Which version do you send to the client?

Where this frees your time — and where it must not

AI hands back hours of drafting, structuring, and first-pass summarising — hours you can spend on the judgement that actually makes you an advisor. But the figures, the causal claims, and the sign-off never delegate. Two honest framings to carry into every report.

+

Where AI can genuinely lighten the load

First-draft, structuring, and phrasing work — draft it fast, then verify every figure.

  • Drafting variance commentary and management-report narrative for you to fact-check
  • Structuring an analysis, suggesting ratios to examine, outlining an advisory memo
  • Rephrasing dense findings into plain client language you then verify
  • Proposing questions to investigate — which you answer from the verified ledger
–

Where the accountant is the point

The numbers, the causes, and the professional sign-off are yours to own.

  • Any figure, percentage, or subtotal — recompute and tie to the source, never trust
  • Any causal "because" — evidence it, or downgrade it to a possible association
  • The professional judgement of what the numbers mean for the client
  • Signing off on a report or advice you have not verified line by line
